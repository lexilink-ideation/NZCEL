# Marking Rubrics — Platform Practice Rubrics for NZCEL (Academic) Levels 4 and 5

> **Status:** DRAFT for review.
>
> **What these rubrics are:** feedback rubrics designed for this preparation platform. Their
> criteria come from the wording of the NZCEL Graduate Profile Outcomes and the definitions in
> the NZQA Supporting Document glossary (SD pp.48–50).
>
> **What they are not:** NZQA marking schedules, unit standard performance criteria, or a
> provider's approved assessment criteria. The SD sets **no** pass marks, cut scores or grade
> boundaries for NZCEL, and these rubrics do not create any. No combination of ratings on this
> platform means "pass", "achieved" or "ready".
>
> Before any task described as "modelled on" an EAP unit standard is rated, compare the
> criteria below with that standard's official performance criteria (VERIFY-02). Where they
> differ, the official criteria take precedence and this file must be updated.

---

## 1. How the rubrics work

### 1.1 Condition checks (recorded before rating, reported separately)

| Check | Source |
|---|---|
| Word count / duration meets the minimum stated for the task (see alignment docs §6) | SD p.30, p.40, p.43 |
| Test conditions respected (timed, locked editor, dictionary only), where required | SD p.22, p.40, p.43 |
| Speaking: only cue cards for presentations; no script for interactions; visuals with minimal text | SD p.40, p.43 |
| Speaking: interaction includes at least one other live participant (for coverage slots) | SD p.40, p.43 |
| Learner declares the work is their own and that no AI tools were used | SD p.21 |

A condition that is not met is reported as **"Condition not met: [which]"**. The performance is
still rated so the learner gets feedback. The coverage tracker shows the slot as *Attempted,
condition not met*.

### 1.2 Descriptor scale

Each criterion is rated on four descriptor bands:

| Band | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Not yet evident** | Little evidence of the criterion in this performance |
| 2 | **Developing** | Some evidence; inconsistent or limited |
| 3 | **Approaching the GPO description** | Mostly consistent with the GPO wording; some gaps |
| 4 | **Consistent with the GPO description in this task** | Evidence in this task matches the GPO wording and glossary definitions |

Rules:
- Bands are **ordinal labels for feedback**, not marks. They are not added, averaged,
  weighted or turned into percentages.
- A band 4 on every criterion is **not** a pass. It means "in this practice task, your
  performance matched the description".
- Raters choose a band and add **one specific comment per criterion** with an example from
  the learner's work.

### 1.3 Rater types

Every rating records its rater type: `teacher`, `peer`, `self` or `automated`. Automated
ratings are labelled "Automated formative feedback" and are never the only rating shown on
a rubric task (blueprint §7).

## 2. Level 4 Academic — Writing (GPO L4A-WRI)

> GPO: *Write detailed, developed, moderately complex academic texts.*
> Glossary (SD p.49): texts of an academic nature that include inter-related facts and/or
> ideas, developed through the text and communicated using complex sentence structures.

Applies to: L4-13, L4-14 (22749-style, test conditions, ≥ 500), L4-15 (22750-style,
researched, ≥ 800), L4-16 (≥ 400).

| Criterion | 1 Not yet evident | 2 Developing | 3 Approaching | 4 Consistent with GPO description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W4.1 Task and academic purpose** | Does not answer the task or genre | Partly answers the task; genre features unclear | Answers the task; genre mostly appropriate (essay/report/summary) | Fully answers the task in an appropriate academic genre |
| **W4.2 Detail and development** *("detailed, developed")* | Ideas are listed or asserted, not developed | Some ideas developed; support is thin or general | Most ideas are developed with explanation and examples | Main ideas are consistently developed with relevant explanation, examples and/or evidence |
| **W4.3 Inter-related ideas and organisation** *("inter-related … developed through the text")* | No clear structure; ideas are unconnected | Paragraphing present; links between ideas often unclear | Clear structure; ideas mostly linked; cohesive devices used with some errors | Logical structure; ideas linked within and between paragraphs; cohesion supports the reader |
| **W4.4 Complex sentence structures** *("communicated using complex sentence structures")* | Mainly simple sentences | Some complex sentences, often with errors that obscure meaning | A range of complex sentences, generally controlled; errors rarely obscure meaning | Complex sentences used purposefully and with control |
| **W4.5 Academic vocabulary** | Mostly general vocabulary; frequent misuse | Some academic vocabulary; noticeable misuse | Appropriate academic vocabulary with occasional misuse | Precise, appropriate academic vocabulary for the topic |
| **W4.6 Use of researched material** *(L4-15 only; 22750-style)* | Sources absent or copied | Sources used, but mostly copied or not acknowledged | Sources paraphrased and acknowledged, with some lapses | Sources integrated in own words, acknowledged consistently, and used to support the writer's points |

Note: the Level 4 writing GPO does not include the word "accurate" (compare Level 5).
Accuracy is therefore judged under W4.4/W4.5 only as far as it affects communication of
meaning.

## 3. Level 5 Academic — Writing (GPO L5A-WRI)

> GPO: *Write well-structured, accurate, evidence-based, extended academic texts on complex and
> technical topics.*
> Glossary terms used: "complex and technical topics" (SD p.48). "Well-structured", "accurate"
> and "evidence-based" are not defined in the SD; the platform's interpretations are given below
> (VERIFY-L5-03).

Applies to: L5-10 (timed, ≥ 600), L5-11 / L5-13 (exit, ≥ 1000), L5-12 (≥ 600).

| Criterion | 1 Not yet evident | 2 Developing | 3 Approaching | 4 Consistent with GPO description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W5.1 Well-structured** | Structure does not suit the genre | Basic structure; sections uneven or poorly signposted | Effective overall structure; some sections less controlled | Structure suits the genre (e.g. case-study report sections, persuasive essay moves); clear signposting throughout |
| **W5.2 Accurate** | Frequent errors impede meaning | Errors are noticeable and sometimes impede meaning | Generally accurate; errors are minor and rarely impede meaning | Consistently accurate grammar, vocabulary and mechanics across an extended text |
| **W5.3 Evidence-based** | Claims are unsupported | Some evidence, often descriptive or not linked to claims | Claims are mostly supported by relevant evidence from sources/data, acknowledged | Claims are consistently supported by relevant, accurately reported evidence, clearly acknowledged and linked to the argument |
| **W5.4 Extended** | Ideas are not sustained | Length reached through repetition rather than development | Argument/analysis mostly sustained across the text | Argument/analysis sustained and developed across the whole text |
| **W5.5 Complex and technical content** | Avoids or misrepresents technical content | Handles technical content superficially | Explains technical content mostly accurately with appropriate specialised vocabulary | Explains complex and technical content accurately, using specialised and academic vocabulary precisely |
| **W5.6 Task fulfilment** | Does not address the task | Partly addresses the task | Addresses all parts with uneven depth | Addresses all parts of the task fully |

**Level 6 boundary:** Level 5 writers are **not** required to synthesise and critique multiple
sources (Level 6 GPO; glossary SD p.49). Do not lower a W5.3 rating because synthesis or
critique is absent.

## 4. Level 4 Academic — Speaking (GPO L4A-SPK)

> GPO: *Participate effectively in sustained spoken discourse in academic contexts.*
> Glossary: participate effectively = clearly presenting ideas and arguments and appropriately
> employing interactive strategies; sustained spoken discourse = interactions maintained for an
> extended time with appropriate turn-taking, and monologues that are developed and well
> sequenced (SD pp.49–50). Fluency = a smooth flow of language with few unnatural pauses (SD p.50).

### 4.1 Presentation (L4-17, 22891-style, ≥ 8 min, followed by Q&A)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 Consistent with GPO description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S4P.1 Developed and well-sequenced content** | Points listed with little development; no clear sequence | Some development; sequence hard to follow in places | Developed content with a mostly clear sequence and signposting | Content developed throughout; clear, logical sequence with effective signposting |
| **S4P.2 Clear presentation of ideas and arguments** | Main message unclear | Main message identifiable; arguments under-supported | Ideas and arguments clear, mostly supported | Ideas and arguments clear and well supported with reasons and examples |
| **S4P.3 Sustaining the monologue** | Cannot sustain; well under time or long breakdowns | Sustains with frequent breakdowns or reading aloud | Sustains for the required time with occasional disruptions | Sustains for the required time with a smooth flow and few unnatural pauses |
| **S4P.4 Responding to questions** | Cannot respond to questions | Responds briefly or partly | Responds relevantly to most questions | Responds relevantly and develops answers |
| **S4P.5 Language range and control** | Limited range; errors often impede meaning | Some range; errors sometimes impede | Adequate academic range; errors rarely impede | Good academic range used with control |
| **S4P.6 Intelligibility** | Often difficult to understand | Understandable with effort | Generally understandable; occasional strain | Consistently understandable |

### 4.2 Interaction (L4-18, L4-19; 6–10 min recommended)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 Consistent with GPO description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S4I.1 Turn-taking strategies** | Rarely takes or yields turns appropriately | Takes turns, but often mistimed or reliant on prompting | Takes and yields turns appropriately most of the time | Uses turn-taking strategies effectively (initiating, interrupting politely, inviting others) |
| **S4I.2 Sustaining the interaction** | Contributions are isolated; interaction breaks down | Interaction is maintained mostly by others | Contributes to maintaining the interaction for the required time | Helps sustain the interaction for the required time, building on others' contributions |
| **S4I.3 Presenting and defending ideas** | Opinions not stated or unsupported | Opinions stated with limited support | Opinions stated and supported; some responses to challenges | States, supports and defends a position; responds to counter-points |
| **S4I.4 Responding to others** | Ignores or misunderstands others | Responds sometimes; misunderstands at times | Responds relevantly to most contributions | Responds relevantly; agrees, disagrees, clarifies and asks for clarification appropriately |
| **S4I.5 Language range, control and intelligibility** | Errors and pronunciation often impede | Sometimes impede | Rarely impede | Controlled, appropriate academic language; consistently understandable |

## 5. Level 5 Academic — Speaking (GPO L5A-SPK)

> GPO: *Participate flexibly and effectively in extended spoken discourse on complex academic
> topics.*
> Glossary: flexibly = adjusting speech and expressions to the situation and recipient and
> adopting appropriate formality; extended spoken discourse = topics sustained and linked across
> a series of longer turns, and monologues where ideas are well developed, extended and presented
> coherently; complex academic topics = requiring some knowledge of a field and relevant academic
> vocabulary (SD pp.48–50).

Applies to: L5-15 (research presentation with Q&A, 10–12 min), L5-16/17 (interaction,
10–12 min). Criteria marked † apply to the presentation only, ‡ to the interaction only.

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 Consistent with GPO description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S5.1 Flexibility (register and formality)** | One register regardless of context | Some adjustment; inappropriate formality at times | Adjusts register for most situations and listeners | Adjusts speech, expressions and formality consistently to situation and listener (e.g. presentation vs. Q&A vs. debate) |
| **S5.2 Extended discourse** | Short, unlinked turns | Longer turns, but ideas not well linked | Longer turns; ideas mostly linked and coherent | Topics sustained and linked across longer turns; monologue well developed and coherent |
| **S5.3 Complex academic content** | Topic handled at a general, non-academic level | Some academic content; terminology often misused | Academic content handled with mostly appropriate terminology | Complex academic content handled accurately with appropriate academic vocabulary |
| **S5.4 Explaining data and supporting arguments with evidence** † | Data/evidence absent or misreported | Data described but not used to support arguments | Data explained and used to support most arguments | Data explained clearly and used consistently to support arguments; sources acknowledged |
| **S5.5 Debate and rebuttal** ‡ | No engagement with opposing views | Acknowledges opposing views without response | Responds to most opposing points with reasons | Responds to opposing arguments directly, with reasoned rebuttal and concession where appropriate |
| **S5.6 Interactive strategies** | Rarely interacts appropriately | Interacts with prompting | Uses interactive strategies appropriately in most exchanges | Uses interactive strategies effectively (turn management, clarification, building on others, handling questions) |
| **S5.7 Fluency** *(glossary SD p.50)* | Frequent unnatural pauses impede communication | Noticeable pauses and reformulation | Generally smooth flow with occasional hesitation | Smooth flow with few unnatural pauses |
| **S5.8 Language range, control and intelligibility** | Often impedes | Sometimes impedes | Rarely impedes | Wide academic range, controlled and consistently understandable |

## 6. Listening and reading: marking keys (auto-marked and teacher-checked)

Listening and reading items are not rated on descriptor bands. Each item has a marking key.

### 6.1 Key requirements
- Correct answer(s), plus accepted variants and paraphrases.
- Justification: transcript timestamp or text paragraph reference.
- Focus ID tag (e.g. `L4A-LIS-SD`), so results report by focus.
- For short-answer items, a note on the meaning required.

### 6.2 Marking principles for constructed responses (platform policy)
- Mark **meaning**, not language accuracy: these items test comprehension, and the SD allows
  responses that are "written, oral, or by non-verbal demonstration" (SD p.40, p.43).
- Accept misspellings unless they create a different word or make the meaning unclear.
- Word limits in items (e.g. "no more than four words") apply only in formative mode unless
  the mock item states them explicitly.
- Answers amended from the learner's own notes after the second play are accepted.
  A third play is never allowed in mock mode (SD p.24).

### 6.3 Reporting
Results are reported as raw counts per focus ID (e.g. "Main ideas 5/6 · Supporting details
7/10 · Organisation 3/4") with item review. There are no totals labelled as levels, and no
pass or fail.

## 7. Rater guidance and calibration

- Read the GPO and glossary definitions for the criterion before rating.
- Rate each criterion independently, using evidence from the performance.
- Teachers: where possible, double-rate a sample and discuss differences. The SD notes providers
  may use internal expertise, moderation and exemplars to assure quality (SD p.29). This
  platform will build an exemplar library in Phase 3.
- Peer and self ratings are for learning. They are shown alongside, not instead of, teacher
  ratings where both exist.
