## 3. Key terms that define the Level 4 standard (from the SD glossary)

The glossary is how this platform separates Level 4 from Levels 3 and 5. Item writers
and rubric users must apply these definitions, not everyday meanings.

| Term in GPO | Glossary definition (paraphrased closely; see source) | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Main ideas | The central thought of a text or a larger section of a text. | SD p.50 |
| Supporting details | Additional information that explains, develops or illustrates the main idea, e.g. reasons, causes, examples. | SD p.50 |
| Moderately complex | Texts that include a mix of straightforward and more complex elements. | SD p.49 |
| Detailed, developed, moderately complex academic texts | Academic texts that include inter-related facts and/or ideas, developed through the text and communicated using complex sentence structures. | SD p.49 |
| Participate effectively | Clearly presenting ideas and arguments and appropriately employing interactive strategies. | SD p.50 |
| Sustained spoken discourse | Interactions maintained for an extended time with appropriate turn-taking strategies, and monologues that are developed and well sequenced. | SD p.49 |

**Note on "specific details":** the Level 3 Academic GPOs target "main ideas and *some
specific details*". The Level 4 GPOs target "main ideas and *supporting details*". The
glossary defines these differently (specific details = discrete items such as dates,
numbers, names; supporting details = reasons, causes, examples; SD p.50). At Level 4,
specific-detail items may appear as *enabling* items, but the evidence weighting must sit
on main ideas and supporting details.

## 4. Platform focus descriptors (derived — not NZQA Learning Outcomes)

The SD explains that Learning Outcomes and Performance Criteria are written by providers
and approved by NZQA (SD p.46). This platform is not a provider, so it **does not claim
to have NZQA Learning Outcomes**. Instead each GPO is split into *focus descriptors*
used only to tag practice items and give feedback. Every descriptor is traceable to a
GPO term or glossary definition.

| Focus ID | Derived from | Practice focus |
|---|---|---|
| L4A-LIS-MI | "main ideas" | Identify the central idea of a whole oral text or of a section of a lecture/discussion |
| L4A-LIS-SD | "supporting details" | Identify reasons, causes, examples and explanations that develop a main idea |
| L4A-LIS-REL | "moderately complex"; "supporting details" | Relate supporting details to the correct main idea; follow signposting across sections |
| L4A-LIS-NOTE | SD p.24 (amending responses from notes); Section 5 L3 writing: "note-taking"; EAP 22892 title ("process information") | Take usable notes while listening, then answer from notes |
| L4A-SPK-PRES | "sustained … monologues that are developed and well sequenced" | Deliver a developed, well-sequenced academic presentation and handle questions |
| L4A-SPK-INT | "participate effectively … interactive strategies"; "turn-taking" | Take part in a sustained academic discussion: turn-taking, agreeing/disagreeing, building on others |
| L4A-SPK-ARG | "clearly presenting ideas and arguments" | State and defend a position with reasons and examples |
| L4A-RDG-MI | "main ideas" | Identify main ideas of an academic text and of its sections |
| L4A-RDG-SD | "supporting details" | Identify reasons, causes, examples, evidence supporting a main idea |
| L4A-RDG-ORG | "moderately complex"; "inter-related" | Follow text organisation (problem–solution, cause–effect, compare–contrast) and how ideas relate |
| L4A-RDG-FIND | Section 5 L4: "short research articles … emphasis on main ideas and findings" | Identify the aim, method in outline, and findings of a simplified research article or abstract |
| L4A-WRI-DEV | "detailed, developed" | Develop ideas with explanation, examples and evidence |
| L4A-WRI-ORG | "inter-related facts and/or ideas … developed through the text" | Organise a structured essay/report with clear paragraphing and cohesion |
| L4A-WRI-LANG | "complex sentence structures" | Use complex sentence structures and academic vocabulary with control |
| L4A-WRI-SRC | EAP US 22750 title: "crafted text using researched material" | Integrate, paraphrase and acknowledge source material |
| L4A-WRI-SUM | Section 5 L4: "summarising academic articles" | Summarise an academic article accurately in own words |

⚠️ **VERIFY-02:** Focus descriptors are a platform design decision. Where a task is
*modelled on* an EAP unit standard, its focus descriptors must be checked against the
performance criteria in that unit standard document (not included in the SD; obtain from
NZQA).

## 5. Academic text types for Level 4 (Section 5, SD p.27)

At Level 4, texts may be any Level 3 academic type "provided they are at the appropriate
level and length", plus the Level 4 additions.

| Skill | Level 3 types (allowed at L4 if at L4 level/length) | Level 4 additions |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | simple academic texts from textbooks on familiar topics; simple infographics/visuals with supporting texts (charts or tables); summaries of articles (simpler versions of longer academic articles); web-based materials; news articles on academic topics | short academic texts explaining concepts or processes; short simple case studies related to academic fields; short research articles (simplified versions of research articles or abstracts with emphasis on main ideas and findings); textbook articles/sections on a specific topic across different fields (e.g. basic economy, biology) |
| Listening | academic lectures; academic discussions; talks/presentations; interviews | panel discussions; documentaries; interviews with experts |
| Speaking | small group work; oral presentations; interactions or negotiations in academic settings (with tutors/advisors or other students); describing visuals | debates/defending an opinion; longer presentations with more detailed information followed by questions and answers |
| Writing | short paragraphs and essays; reports; summaries; reflective journals; note-taking | structured essays; summarising academic articles |

The platform's `text_type` field must use one of these values for every Level 4 item.

| **EAP US 22892** must be one of the texts, providing evidence for the GPO at exit level | Must | SD p.40 |

## 8. Item-level alignment record (required for every question and task)

```yaml
id: L4-RDG-U03-T2-Q05        # level-skill-unit-task-item
level: 4                     # 4 | 5
qualification_ref: 1883
skill: reading               # listening | speaking | reading | writing
gpo_id: L4A-RDG
focus_ids: [L4A-RDG-SD, L4A-RDG-ORG]
mode: mock                   # formative | mock | rubric
task_type: L4-07             # row in matrix §7
text_type: "short academic texts explaining concepts or processes"   # Section 5 value
topic_id: T04
source_text_words: 912       # reading/writing prompts
audio_seconds: null          # listening/speaking
models_unit_standard: null   # e.g. "EAP 22751" — preparation only
evidence_weight: exit        # exit | progress  (SD: other tasks "can be relatively simpler")
sd_refs: ["SD p.27", "SD p.40"]
review_status: draft         # draft | reviewed | approved
```

Validation rules enforced in CI (planned):

1. `gpo_id` must exist and match `level` and `skill`.
2. `text_type` must appear in the Section 5 list for that level and skill.
3. Mock listening: `audio_seconds` ≥ 360 (6 min); a 22892-modelled task ≥ 480 (8 min).
4. Mock reading: `source_text_words` ≥ 800; a 22751-modelled task ≥ 1500.
5. Writing prompts state the minimum word count required by §6.3.
6. Speaking tasks state the minimum duration required by §6.4.
7. A Level 4 item may not use a Level 5-only text type (§5) unless flagged `review_status: draft`.
