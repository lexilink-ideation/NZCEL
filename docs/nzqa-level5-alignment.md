Level 1-to-4 unit standards…", SD p.6; Section 8 tables, SD pp.52–59). The Level 5
Academic sufficiency guidance (SD p.43) names **no mandatory EAP unit standards**.

Consequences for this platform:
- No Level 5 task is labelled "modelled on" a unit standard.
- No Level 5 credit values are stated anywhere on the platform.
- **VERIFY-L5-01:** confirm with NZQA / the qualification specification that no unit
  standards are mandatory for 1884.

## 3. Key terms that define the Level 5 standard

| Term in GPO | Glossary definition (paraphrased closely) | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Complex texts | Texts with inter-related facts and/or ideas developed through the text and communicated using complex sentence structures. | SD p.49 |
| Extended spoken discourse | Interactions where topics are sustained and linked across a series of longer turns; monologues where ideas are well developed, extended and presented coherently. | SD p.49 |
| Participate flexibly | Adjusting speech and expressions to the situation and the recipient, and adopting a level of formality appropriate to the circumstances. | SD p.50 |
| Participate effectively | Clearly presenting ideas and arguments and appropriately employing interactive strategies. | SD p.50 |
| Complex academic topics | Topics requiring some knowledge of a particular academic field and relevant academic vocabulary. | SD p.48 |
| Complex and technical topics | Topics requiring some knowledge of a particular academic field and relevant academic **and specialised** vocabulary. | SD p.48 |
| Wide range of topics *(used in L5 General, not Academic)* | Topics beyond those of immediate relevance or familiarity. | SD p.48 |

**Terms in the Level 5 GPOs that the glossary does not define:** *analyse*, *evaluate*,
*evidence-based*, *well-structured*, *accurate*, *concrete*, *abstract*, *technical*
(as applied to listening), *range of topics*. The platform's working definitions are given
in §4 and are marked as platform interpretations. **VERIFY-L5-03.**

The Section 3 framework mapping for Level 5 Academic reading gives high B2 / CLB 9 /
GSE 67–75 and the CEFR descriptor about reading articles and reports on contemporary
problems in which writers adopt particular stances or viewpoints (SD p.18). This supports
including stance and viewpoint in Level 5 reading.

### Level 5 boundary rules (what not to require)

To keep the platform's Level 5 honest about what Level 5 is, items **must not** require:
- *critical evaluation* of listening texts (the Level 6 listening GPO: "understand and
  critically evaluate", SD p.10);
- *synthesis and critique* of sources in writing (the Level 6 writing GPO, defined in the
  glossary, SD p.49). Level 5 writing is *evidence-based*: it uses and acknowledges
  evidence. It does not have to synthesise or critique sources.

## 5. Academic text types for Level 5 (Section 5, SD p.27)

Level 5 may use all Level 3 and Level 4 academic types "provided they are at the
appropriate level and length", plus:

| Skill | Level 5 additions | Inherited (examples) |
|---|---|---|
| Reading | academic journal articles; research papers/reports; textbook chapters; critical reviews | L4 research articles, case studies, textbook sections |
| Listening | seminars/webinars; podcasts; panel debates | L3/L4 lectures, panel discussions, documentaries, interviews with experts |
| Speaking | academic debates; presentation on research, explaining data and supporting arguments with evidence | L4 debates/defending an opinion, presentations with Q&A |
| Writing | persuasive essays; research summaries; formal academic reports on case studies; critical reviews | L4 structured essays, summaries of academic articles |

**Platform rule:** a Level 5 **mock** or **exit-weight rubric task** must use a Level 5
addition, or an inherited type at Level 5 length and complexity. Inherited types are
allowed only in formative mode or as `evidence_weight: progress` tasks.

## 6. Sufficiency requirements — Level 5 Academic (SD p.43; summary SD p.30)

### 6.1 Listening
| Requirement | Type | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum **two** oral texts, on **two different topics** relevant to the academic context, **separate occasions** | Must | SD p.43 |
| At least one task provides evidence at exit level; others may be simpler (progress evidence) | Must | SD p.43 |
| Each text approximately **8–10 minutes** | Recommended | SD p.43, p.30 |
| Listening texts **repeated once only** | Must | SD p.43 |
| Visuals allowed but no large amounts of written text | Must | SD p.43 |

### 6.2 Reading
| Requirement | Type | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum **two** texts, **two different topics**, academic context, **separate occasions** | Must | SD p.43 |
| One at exit level; others may be simpler | Must | SD p.43 |
| Texts **minimum 1500 words** | Recommended | SD p.43, p.30 |
| Responses written, oral or non-verbal; visuals permitted | Permitted | SD p.43 |

### 6.3 Writing
| Requirement | Type | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum **two** texts, **two different topics**, academic context, **separate occasions** | Must | SD p.43 |
| One at exit level; others may be simpler | Must | SD p.43 |
| Exit-level text **minimum 1000 words**; other text(s) **minimum 600 words** | Recommended | SD p.43, p.30 |
| **One text assessed under test conditions** | Must | SD p.43 |

⚠️ The SD does not say *which* text is under test conditions or give a time limit.
Platform choice (marked as such): the 600-word text is the test-conditions task. The
1000-word exit text is a crafted, source-based task. **VERIFY-L5-04.**

### 6.4 Speaking
| Requirement | Type | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum **two** tasks, **two different topics**, academic context, **separate occasions** | Must | SD p.43 |
| One at exit level; others may be simpler | Must | SD p.43 |
| Interactions and monologues **10–12 minutes** | Recommended | SD p.43, p.30 |
| **At least one interaction and one presentation** | Must | SD p.43 |
| Minimal reference to written materials; cue cards for presentations; no script-like support | Must | SD p.43 |

### 6.5 Cross-skill conditions
As for Level 4 (SD pp.20–24): no required sequence; integrated approach recommended;
dictionaries permitted (dictionary function only); independence and authenticity; GenAI
restricted in assessment.

Level 5 validation rules (planned CI checks):
1. Mock listening `audio_seconds` ≥ 480 (8 min).
2. Mock reading `source_text_words` ≥ 1500.
3. Writing exit task states ≥ 1000 words; other writing tasks state ≥ 600 words.
4. Speaking rubric tasks state 10–12 minutes.
5. Mock and exit-weight items use a Level 5 text type, or an inherited type at Level 5 length (§5).
6. No item's answer key requires critical evaluation of a *listening* text, or synthesis
   and critique of multiple sources in *writing* (Level 6 boundary, §4).

## 9. Level 4 → Level 5 differentiation summary

| Skill | Level 4 Academic | Level 5 Academic | What changes in platform items |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | Main ideas + supporting details; *moderately complex*; 6–8 min (22892 ≥ 8) | *Extended complex*; concrete, abstract **and technical**; 8–10 min | L5 adds argument tracking across long texts, technical vocabulary in context, abstract topics, stance |
| Reading | Main ideas + supporting details; *moderately complex*; ≥ 800 (22751 ≥ 1500) | Understand **+ analyse + evaluate**; *complex*; range of topics; ≥ 1500 | L5 adds analysis and evaluation items; texts are journal articles, research reports, chapters, critical reviews |
| Writing | *Detailed, developed, moderately complex*; 3 texts (≥ 500 test, ≥ 800 crafted, ≥ 400) | Well-structured, **accurate, evidence-based, extended**; complex **and technical**; 2 texts (≥ 1000 exit, ≥ 600; one under test) | L5 rubric weights accuracy and use of evidence more heavily; longer texts; technical topics |
| Speaking | Participate *effectively* in *sustained* discourse; 6–10 min (22891 ≥ 8); ≥ 1 interaction | Participate **flexibly** and effectively in **extended** discourse on *complex academic* topics; 10–12 min; ≥ 1 interaction **and** ≥ 1 presentation | L5 rubric adds register flexibility; longer, linked turns; research/data presentation |
